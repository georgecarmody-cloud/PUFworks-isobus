See & Spray CAN Bus ΓÇö Electrical Theory of Operation
Component Information
Controller Area Network binary unit system (CAN bus) systems transfer messages between every control
unit on the machine. These messages include commands, requests, broadcast information, responses,
acknowledgements, and various group functions. The CAN bus is a linear network, and the control units
are connected along its length in series.
The operator has no direct control of the CAN bus systems. CAN bus operates automatically whenever the
key switch is in the ACC, RUN, or START positions.
Can Circuits
Digital messages transfer information from control unit to control unit along two circuits. One control unit
sends a message, and that message is either addressed to another control unit or to all control units. The
receiving control unit uses the information in the message to monitor or control the machine.
Each CAN bus network includes a 2.5-3.5 V CAN Hi circuit (yellow wire) and a 1.5-2.5 V CAN Lo circuit (green
wire). When no messages are being transmitted on the CAN bus, CAN bus high and CAN bus low circuits
are both approximately 2.5 V. When messages are being sent between the control units, the CAN bus high
voltage increases, and the CAN bus low voltage decreases.

See & Spray Ethernet and FAKRA ΓÇö Electrical Theory of 
Operation
Component Information
See & Spray Cameras and FAKRA Cables
Each See & Spray camera sends video information to a vision processing unit (VPU), using a non-repairable
Fachkreis Automobil (FAKRA) cable with integrated non-repairable connectors. FAKRA cables allow up to 6
GHz of information to be transmitted. FAKRA connectors mechanically lock to ensure a secure connection.
More See & Spray camera information, including how and when to calibrate these cameras, is provided in
the appropriate OperatorΓÇÖs Manual (OM).
VPUs
VPUs 1, 2, 5, and 6 connect to eight FAKRA cables and eight See & Spray cameras respectively. VPU 3
connects to four FAKRA cables and four See & Spray cameras. Each VPU processes the necessary
information and sends that information along to the appropriate See & Spray Ethernet switch, using an
Ethernet cable. The VPU periodically verifies that all of these FAKRA and Ethernet connections are
functioning correctly.
Each VPU also communicates necessary camera information through the BoomTrac CAN bus to the
gateway control unit (GWC) and through the ExactApply CAN bus to the master nozzle control unit (MNC).
This camera data provides the information necessary to automatically adjust the ExactApply nozzles (NZCs).
See & Spray Ethernet Switches and Ethernet Cables
See & Spray Ethernet switch 1 and 2, connects to four VPUs through four Ethernet cables. See & Spray
Ethernet switch 1 and 2 process the necessary information and act as a hub for See and Spray camera data.
Twisted pair circuits provide extra signal protection from magnetic influences. Ethernet cables provide
high-speed communication, which allows real-time adjustments. When needed, these Ethernet cables
provide the means to reprogram the VPUs. These Ethernet cables and integrated connectors are non-
repairable.
Ethernet Configuration 2
See & Spray Ethernet switch 1 & 2 transmits all of the See & Spray camera data information along to
configuration 2 Ethernet switch assembly, using two twisted pair Ethernet cables.
Configuration 2 Ethernet switch assembly also receives camera data from the right-hand camera and the
left-hand cameras when equipped on the machine. Each connection is made with a twisted pair of circuits.
This information plus the See & Spray camera information is sent to configuration 1 Ethernet switch
assembly, using two twisted pair Ethernet cables.

GWC ΓÇö Electrical Theory of Operation
The gateway control unit (GWC) enables the See & Spray application within the display. The GWC is the
bridge between the implement controller area network (CAN) binary unit system (bus) and the See & Spray
components. If the GWC is offline, or not on the machine's implement CAN bus, the See & Spray page and
run page modules are grayed out with See & Spray disabled.
Component Information:
Unswitched power is a constant 12 V power supply that comes directly from the batteries and battery
disconnect switch. A system fuse within the battery box protects the supplied power circuit to the rear mid-
chassis cab load center. The MNC/See & Spray control unit fuse within the rear mid-chassis load center
protects the supplied power circuit to the ExactApply load center. Power is then supplied to the See &
Spray load center.
Unswitched power is provided through the gateway and Ethernet switch power fuse within the See & Spray
load center.
Switched power for the system to wake up the control units, as well as begin the proper shutdown
procedure of the control units, comes from the power train domain control unit (PODC). The PODC receives
the wakeup signal from the operator station domain control unit (OSDC). The PODC then sends 12 V to the
GWC. The GWC then relays wakeup and shutdown signals to the Ethernet switch and the vision processing
unit(s) (VPUs) through the GWC.
The GWC acts as a gateway to connect the implement CAN bus and See & Spray/BoomTrac CAN bus. The
VPUs' vision spray modules (VSMs) communicate status messages to the GWC. The GWC monitors
messages on both busses and relays the necessary information to necessary control units on the
implement bus.
The GWC allows the operator interface with VPUs VSMs and cameras on the boom through the See & Spray
screen on the display. The user can select a specific VPU VSM and camera lens. It also provides visual
feedback to what the camera is sensing by sending the information from the VPU and broadcasting the
video through ethernet from VPU to the ethernet switches and then back to the Generation 4 Display.
